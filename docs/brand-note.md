# Daybook - Brand Note

*A living reference for how Daybook thinks, sounds and looks, and who it's for.
Draft 2026-10-06. Edit freely as it sharpens.*

---

## The one line

**Daybook turns the mess of a whole life into actionable insight - calmly, privately, and shared with the people who live it with you.**

## The north star (felt, never declared)

The real aim is to help you **live your best life - a life well lived.** It shapes
every detail, but we almost never say it out loud. People should *feel* the intent
in how the thing behaves and breathes, not be told it. The tagline "For a life well
lived" stays a footer whisper, never a billboard.

## The promise under everything

**Actionable insight.** You offload your life into Daybook; it surfaces what matters
when it matters and hands it back as insight you can *act on* - progress at a glance,
a clear next step, your own life made legible. Not data for data's sake. Data that
tells you what to do next, and quietly makes you look on top of it all.

---

## Positioning

The rival story (e.g. TidyPolar) is clean but small: *mess → projects → AI tidies it.*
Functional admin. Plastic buckets.

Ours is bigger - and, crucially, **true**, because the depth is actually built:

> **A tool for living more consciously - that clears your admin too.**

The admin (mail, calendar, notes, tasks, money, joined up) is the *price of entry*,
done calmly. The soul is rhythm, reflection, well-being, and a life organised around
what actually matters. Not "an admin tool with journaling bolted on" - the reverse.

**The moat is depth and detail.** Every corner is worked through. A marketing story
can be copied in an afternoon; a deeply considered product can't be faked. The
look-and-feel work isn't decoration - it's the depth made visible in the first three
seconds. Shallow products can't survive that level of finish; ours can.

## The five pillars

1. **Ease** - the everyday admin lifted. Mail, calendar, notes, tasks and money, under
   one calm roof and joined up. Get your stuff done with the minimum hassle.
2. **It comes to you** - Daybook is *temporal*. It surfaces the right thing when it's
   due and works to *your* rhythms - tasks resurface when they matter, people come
   round on a cadence you set, reviews arrive when you asked, the day lays itself out.
   You flow with your life instead of chasing it. His buckets just sit there; ours moves.
3. **A second brain → actionable insight** - find anything the instant you need it, and
   see your life as insight you can act on: progress graphs, expandable detail,
   Information-is-Beautiful dashboards. Offload with confidence, resurface with trust.
4. **Life areas, shared** - not folders, not plastic buckets: the real parts of a life
   (health, money, the kids, the move, work), each with its own notes, tasks, goals and
   people - and you can **share an area with the people inside it.** Co-parents on *The
   kids*, a family on *The move*, a group on an event, a couple on the money. *This is
   the one thing the "projects" crowd can't touch - make the most of it.*
5. **A more conscious life** - vision, goals and gentle reviews; journalling, rhythms,
   a quiet well-being corner. Daybook helps you notice what matters and actually tend to
   it - without ever insisting you audit your soul on a Sunday.

**"Life areas" vs "projects":** same model. A life area *is* a project. Give one a
deadline and it runs as a project, counting you down. One identity, two moods - no fork.

## Who it's for

**Beachhead, not a wall.** The product is universal; the marketing wins a beating heart
of early fans first.

- **Primary wedge:** the household's quiet operations chief - very often a **mother** -
  who holds everyone's appointments, the admin, the emotional weather *and* her own inner
  life. She isn't running four tools; she's running a whole life, for more than one person.
  Shareable life areas and well-being depth land harder for her than for anyone.
- **How to play it:** be *unmistakably for her* in tone, story, imagery and channels -
  and in holistic touches (e.g. a gentle cycle/rhythm view) - while keeping the product
  genuinely for everyone. Show, don't label: never "a women's app" (limiting, dated).
  Like Notion winning students first, then the world.
- **Also lands for:** anyone wanting to live more consciously while staying effortlessly
  on top - freelancers, couples, carers, the quietly overwhelmed.

**Persona sketch - "Marta, 41":** two kids, part-time work, aging parents, a house move
on the horizon. Her life lives in six apps, three group chats and her head. Daybook gives
*The kids*, *The move*, *Work* and *Me* each a home; the co-parent sees only *The kids*;
the week's deadlines surface themselves; and once a month a two-minute review shows her,
in a simple graph, that she's actually keeping up. She looks - and feels - on top of it.

## AI & privacy stance (an ownable differentiator)

- **Works beautifully without AI.** The whole product runs AI-off. AI is seasoning, not
  the foundation.
- **Used sparingly, only where it earns its place** - drafting a reply, reading a flyer,
  a coaching prompt. Every use has its own switch.
- **Nothing is ever sent without you.** Private by design: no ads, no selling, no noise.
- Why it matters: a second brain that *needs* AI is hostage to a model, a subscription and
  a privacy question. Ours isn't. In a market drunk on AI, restraint is trust.

---

## Tone of voice

- Calm, warm, unhurried, quietly confident. A trusted friend with good taste - never a
  productivity drill sergeant, never twee, never hype.
- Plain words for real life ("the kids", "the move"), not system-speak.
- Show the soul, don't preach it. The ethos is felt, not announced.
- Active, specific, generous. Short sentences that breathe.

## Visual & design principles

- **Warm, Zen-ish palette** (shared with Dojo Zen de Lisboa): warm paper, soft inks,
  terracotta/rust accent, gold. Serif display (**Fraunces** on the site, **Cormorant** in
  app) over a clean body face.
- **A whole, not a stack of boxes.** Soft, generously-padded panels in a grid; real
  whitespace; quiet layered depth. The craft bar is TidyPolar - in our own palette.
- **Colour as a whisper** - a dot, a keyline, a faint wash - never a tint smeared over
  everything. Premium = restraint.
- **Dashboards & insight are a signature.** Metrics, progress graphs, expandable "bits
  that open for more", Information-is-Beautiful views. Actionable insight, made visual.
- **A touch of Zen soul** - a soft hue wash behind a title, a faint seal/mark, the odd
  quiet character; imagery muted so it blends into the ground.
- **Rules:** large, legible text (Robin's eyesight). **No full-width buttons** (slim pill
  or icon); ideally no empty full-width rows at all - fill space with grids/columns.
  Every titled box collapsible. Light and dark both designed, never an afterthought.

## Marketing funnel - first sketch

- **Story angles:** the mental load made visible; "share the area, not the group chat";
  the two-minute review that proves you're keeping up; living consciously without the cult;
  privacy & AI-restraint as a stance.
- **Content:** short, warm pieces and visuals built around real life areas (the move, the
  new baby, the aging parent, the side project) - each showing surfacing + insight + sharing.
  A holistic/rhythm angle (incl. cycle) for the primary wedge.
- **Channels:** invite-only early access (scarcity + care); word-of-mouth from the shared-area
  moment (sharing *is* the growth loop - you invite the people in your life); communities
  around parenting, well-being and conscious living.
- **Proof:** the product's own finish is the pitch - a beautiful, deep app that makes you
  look good. Let people *feel* the depth; it's the thing that can't be copied.
