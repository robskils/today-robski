Ways of working

Kaya at speed

November 2026 · Feito em Lisboa · 4 min read

Kaya is built to get out of your way. Once a handful of keys are in your hands, it moves as fast as you can think - no reaching for the mouse, no hunting through menus. Here is the fast way round.

Speed is not really about doing more, faster. It is about friction - the small taxes a tool charges on every little thing you do. Reach for the mouse, find the button, wait for the page to settle. Each one is tiny; together they are the reason an open laptop can feel like work before you have done any. Take the friction away and the tool seems to disappear, which is exactly what you want from the place your whole life lives.

One key does most of it

If you learn a single shortcut, make it ⌘K. It opens Search, and Search reaches everything - your notes, tasks, people, events and Spaces. Start typing and the thing you want rises to the top; press Return and you are there. You rarely need to navigate Kaya at all. You think of a thing, and you summon it. (On Windows, Ctrl stands in for ⌘ throughout.)

Think of a thing, and summon it. ⌘K is most of Kaya.

Making things, without breaking stride

The other keys worth knowing start something new, so a thought never has to wait for your hands to find the right corner of the screen:

  • ⌘N makes a new note - a blank page, ready to type.
  • ⌥⌘N opens the full new-task box, over whatever you are on.
  • ⌘E brings your whole inbox up, from anywhere.
  • ⌘; opens the Notepad - your running scratch page, always as you left it.
  • ⌘T opens a second thing beside the first, so you can keep an email open while you write a note.
  • ⌘Z undoes it - yes, even deleting a note.
  • Esc backs you out of whatever is open.

On a phone

A phone has no keyboard to lean on, so the shortcut there is the gold + at the foot of the screen. Tap it and the same New menu opens - a note, a task, an event, whatever you like - and the right box opens for it. One tap to anything you make.

Speed in service of calm

None of this is about being busier. It is the opposite. When the tool keeps up with you, you stop managing the tool and get back to the thing you opened it for. The fewer seconds Kaya costs you, the more it earns its place - quietly, quickly, and on your side.
