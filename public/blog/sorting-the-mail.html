Sorting the mail

November 2026 · Feito em Lisboa · 4 min read

Most inboxes are a single heap: read and unread, urgent and idle, all in one endless scroll. Kaya treats mail as a short series of small decisions instead. Everything new lands in one pile - the Inbox, your "to sort" - and from there each email goes to exactly one of four places. Clear the pile and you are done; there is nothing hiding further down.

One pile to sort

New mail arrives in the Inbox, and that is the only pile that means "I have not decided about this yet". So its count is the one number worth watching - it is work still to do, not a measure of how busy you are. When the Inbox is empty, your mail is handled, however much arrived.

You are not sorting by reading every word, either. A glance at the sender and the subject is usually enough to know where a message belongs. The reading can wait for the pile it lands in.

Four places, four keys

When an email is open, the keys 1 to 4 send it where it belongs, and Kaya moves you straight on to the next one - so a morning's mail is a handful of keystrokes, not a scroll.

  • Urgent (1) - needs you today.
  • Important (2) - matters, but not this minute.
  • Read later (3) - worth your eyes in a quiet moment, never your morning.
  • Archive (4) - read, done, kept. Nothing is deleted unless you ask; it is simply out of the way, and still there when you search.

Sort one and the next opens, so the pile empties under your hands.

Mail that becomes something

An email is often really a task, a date, or a note you will want later. Kaya lets you turn it into one, in place:

  • Turn into Task - the email becomes a task, with a link back to the thread.
  • Turn into Event - put it on the calendar. When the email mentions dates, a "Dates found" line offers them up, so you never retype a time.
  • Turn into Note - keep the content as a note, filed in the right Space.

The mail does not have to live in your inbox to be remembered. Once it has become the thing it was really asking for, you can archive it with a clear conscience.

Why four, and not forty

Folders multiply. Give yourself twenty and every email becomes a filing decision - and so you stop filing. Four is few enough to choose at a glance and enough to tell today's fire from next week's reading. The point was never a perfect taxonomy; it is an empty pile and a quiet mind.

A full inbox is not a to-do list - it is an un-sorted one. Make the four small decisions, let the urgent surface and the rest settle, and the inbox goes quiet.

Connect your mailbox - Gmail, Google Workspace, Yahoo or any IMAP account - and the first time you clear the pile to nothing, you will wonder why mail ever felt like a wall.
